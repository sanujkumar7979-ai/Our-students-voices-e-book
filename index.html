OUR STUDENTS VOICE

1..... Say No to Heavy School Bags

We, as students, have an important request to our government regarding the heavy school bags that students carry every day. School education is an essential part of our lives, but carrying a very heavy bag to school has become a serious concern for many students. A school bag should contain only the things that are necessary for learning, but nowadays students often have to carry several textbooks, notebooks, practical files, water bottles, lunch boxes, and other materials. As a result, our school bags become extremely heavy. Carrying a heavy bag every day can put unnecessary pressure on a student's shoulders, neck, and back. It can also make students feel tired before they even reach the classroom. Students should be able to concentrate on their studies and participate in school activities without feeling uncomfortable because of the weight of their bags. Technology has changed the way we learn. Many countries around the world, including countries such as Australia, Japan, and the United States, have adopted or are increasingly using digital learning tools such as laptops, tablets, and digital textbooks in education. These devices can store many books and educational materials in one place. Instead of carrying several heavy books and notebooks, students could use a suitable digital device for a large part of their schoolwork. Therefore, we request our government to consider making a rule or policy to reduce the weight of school bags. Schools could gradually introduce laptops or tablets for students wherever it is practical and affordable. Digital textbooks, notes, assignments, and educational resources could be provided through these devices. This would reduce the amount of physical material students need to carry every day. However, we understand that not every student may have access to expensive technology. Therefore, the government should ensure that this system is affordable and accessible to all students, especially students from economically weaker families. Schools could provide devices through government-supported programmes, shared digital facilities, or other suitable methods. We are not asking for books to disappear completely. Books are still valuable for learning, reading, writing, and developing important skills. Our request is simply to find a better balance between traditional learning and modern technology. We hope our government will listen to the concerns of students and take practical steps to reduce the weight of school bags. Education should help students grow, learn, and stay healthy—not make them physically uncomfortable. Therefore, our humble request to the government is:

Please make suitable rules to reduce the burden of heavy school bags and encourage the use of laptops, tablets, and other digital learning resources wherever possible.

A lighter bag can mean a healthier, happier, and more comfortable student.


2..... Let Students Choose Useful Subjects

We, as students, have another important request regarding the education system. We believe that students should have more freedom to choose subjects according to their interests, abilities, and future goals. At present, many students are required to study a large number of subjects, even when some of those subjects may not be directly connected to their future education or career plans. Students may have to study around 10 to 14 different subjects, depending on their school and curriculum. Studying many subjects at the same time can create a heavy academic workload. It can also take away valuable time that students could use to understand the subjects that are more important for their chosen field. For example, a student who wants to become an engineer may need to focus more on Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Computer Science, and other related areas. Similarly, a student interested in medicine may need to give greater attention to Biology, Chemistry, and Physics. A student interested in business may benefit more from subjects such as Economics, Mathematics, Accountancy, and Business Studies. This does not mean that general knowledge or basic subjects are useless. Subjects such as General Knowledge, Moral Education, and other foundational areas can certainly have value. However, students should not be forced to spend a large amount of time on subjects that have little connection with their interests or future goals. Instead, schools could provide a common foundation of essential subjects and then allow students to choose additional subjects according to their interests. For example, students could choose from areas such as Computer Science, Coding, Financial Literacy, Communication Skills, Artificial Intelligence, Entrepreneurship, Environmental Studies, or other practical subjects. Such a system could help students develop useful skills while also reducing unnecessary academic pressure. It would allow students to spend more time learning deeply rather than simply trying to complete the syllabus of a large number of subjects. We understand that education should provide students with broad knowledge and help them become responsible citizens. Therefore, we are not asking schools to remove every subject that students find difficult or less interesting. Instead, we request a balanced education system in which students have some choice and can focus more on subjects that are relevant to their future. Therefore, our humble request to the government is:

Please create suitable guidelines so that schools do not unnecessarily force students to study a large number of subjects. Give students more freedom to choose subjects according to their interests, abilities, and future goals, while ensuring that every student receives a strong basic education.

Education should not only be about studying more subjects. It should be about learning better, developing useful skills, discovering our interests, and preparing ourselves for the future.


3.....We Want to Study Artificial Intelligence

We, as students, have another important request regarding our education system. We want Artificial Intelligence, or AI, to become an important part of education in schools, colleges, and universities. We are living in a rapidly changing world. Artificial Intelligence is becoming a part of many areas of our lives, including education, healthcare, business, transportation, communication, science, and technology. New AI tools and technologies are being developed every day. Because of this rapid development, students need to understand AI and learn how to use it responsibly and effectively. Many students spend a large amount of their time memorising information from textbooks and preparing for examinations. Books are certainly valuable, and we are not saying that books should completely disappear. However, education should also prepare students for the world in which they will live and work. If technology is changing so quickly, our education system should change along with it. Learning AI from an early stage could help students understand important concepts such as machine learning, coding, robotics, data, automation, and responsible use of technology. Students could also learn how AI can be used to solve real-world problems. This could encourage creativity, innovation, and problem-solving skills. AI education could also help students prepare for future careers. As technology develops, many new jobs and professions related to AI and technology are emerging. At the same time, some existing jobs may change because of automation and AI. Therefore, students should have the opportunity to develop the skills needed to work alongside these technologies. We also believe that simply using AI is not enough. Students should learn about its advantages, limitations, safety, ethics, privacy, and responsible use. They should understand that AI is a tool created by humans and should be used carefully rather than blindly depending on it. However, we do not believe that every traditional subject should immediately be removed. Mathematics, science, languages, communication, and other basic subjects provide important foundations for learning AI and understanding the world. Instead, AI education should be introduced as a major part of the curriculum, with students gradually getting more opportunities to specialise in it. Therefore, our humble request to the government is:

Please make suitable rules and policies to ensure that every student gets proper AI education in schools, colleges, and universities. Give students the opportunity to learn AI alongside essential foundational subjects and provide practical training so that they are prepared for the future.

The future is being shaped by technology, and students are the future of our country. If we learn AI today, we can become creators and innovators tomorrow—not just users of technology. We do not want education that only prepares us for yesterday's world. We want education that prepares us for tomorrow.

4..... We Do Not Want to Study Letter Writing

We, as students, have another important request regarding our education system. We believe that the way we communicate has changed significantly because of modern technology. Today, people communicate through smartphones, WhatsApp, email, video calls, messaging applications, and many other digital platforms. Because communication has become faster and more convenient, we believe that traditional letter writing should no longer be a compulsory part of the school curriculum. In the past, letters were one of the main ways people communicated with relatives, friends, offices, and government departments. People had to write a letter, put it into an envelope, attach a stamp, and send it through the postal system. It could take several days or even weeks for the message to reach the other person. Today, communication is completely different. A message can be sent through WhatsApp or another messaging platform within seconds. Emails can be used for formal communication, applications, documents, and professional messages. Video calls allow people to communicate face-to-face even when they are in different cities or countries. Because of these changes, we question why students should spend a large amount of valuable study time learning traditional letter-writing formats that they may rarely use in their everyday lives. We are not saying that students should stop learning how to communicate properly. Communication skills are extremely important. However, students should be taught modern forms of communication that are useful in today's world. They should learn how to write professional emails, digital messages, applications, online forms, reports, presentations, and other forms of modern communication. Students should also learn digital communication etiquette. They should understand how to communicate respectfully online, how to protect personal information, how to identify suspicious messages, how to avoid scams, and how to communicate professionally through email and other digital platforms. We also believe that traditional letters can still be introduced as part of history, culture, or optional learning. Students should know what letters are and how communication worked in the past. However, making detailed traditional letter-writing formats compulsory for every student may not be the most useful use of their limited study time. Every student's time is valuable. Students already have many subjects, assignments, examinations, projects, and activities. Therefore, the education system should focus on knowledge and skills that prepare students for modern life and future careers. Our humble request to the government is:

Please make suitable rules so that schools do not unnecessarily force students to study traditional letter-writing formats as a compulsory subject. Instead, give greater importance to modern communication skills such as email writing, professional digital communication, online applications, presentations, and responsible use of messaging platforms.

.

We respect traditional methods of communication and understand their historical importance. However, education should evolve with society and technology. We want to learn how to communicate effectively in today's world—not only how people communicated in the past. Our time is precious, and education should help us use that time to develop skills that will be useful throughout our lives.


5..... Not Want to Study History

We, as students, have another important request regarding our education system. We believe that the curriculum should change according to the needs of the modern world. One subject that we would like the government and education authorities to reconsider is History. History teaches us about the past. It can help us understand important events, cultures, societies, inventions, movements, and the development of different countries. We respect the importance of learning about our country's history, because knowing about India's past can help students understand how our society and nation developed. However, we believe that students are sometimes required to study a very large amount of historical information, including numerous dates, battles, rulers, events, and details about countries and civilizations around the world. For many students, memorising such information can take a significant amount of study time. Our question is not whether history has any value. Our question is whether every student needs to study such a large amount of historical information as a compulsory subject when the world is changing so quickly. Today, Artificial Intelligence is transforming many areas of life. AI is being used in education, healthcare, science, business, transportation, communication, agriculture, engineering, and many other fields. Students who understand AI may be better prepared to participate in the technological future. Therefore, we propose that some of the time currently dedicated to detailed History education could be used for a new subject focused on Artificial Intelligence and modern technology. An AI subject could teach students about machine learning, coding, robotics, data, automation, generative AI, digital safety, AI ethics, and practical problem-solving. Students could learn how AI systems work and how to use them responsibly. They could also create projects and develop solutions to real-world problems. We are not suggesting that students should completely forget India's history or the important lessons of the past. A basic understanding of Indian history, culture, independence, constitutional development, and major historical events can remain part of education. However, students could have greater flexibility regarding how much detailed history they study. Instead of spending many hours memorising information that may not be relevant to a student's future career, students could use some of that time to develop modern technological skills. Therefore, our humble request to the government is:

Please review the current curriculum and consider replacing some of the compulsory detailed History curriculum with a dedicated Artificial Intelligence and Modern Technology subject. Students should learn the essential history of India and the world while also receiving strong, practical education in AI and emerging technologies.The past helps us understand where we came from, but education must also prepare us for where we are going.

. We respect history, but we also want to be prepared for the future. We want an education system that teaches us not only about yesterday, but also about tomorrow.


6..... Not Want to Study Mathematics

We, as students, have another important request regarding our education system. We believe that Mathematics is useful in many areas of life, but we also believe that every student should not be forced to study advanced Mathematics for many years when it is not connected to their future goals. Mathematics is certainly an important subject. Basic mathematical skills such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, percentages, measurements, money management, and simple calculations are necessary in everyday life. However, after learning these essential skills, students should have more freedom to decide how deeply they want to study Mathematics. Many students have different interests and career goals. Some students want to become doctors, engineers, scientists, programmers, artists, designers, entrepreneurs, writers, musicians, athletes, or professionals in other fields. The mathematical requirements of these careers can be very different. For example, a student who wants to become a professional artist or a writer may not need to study advanced algebra, trigonometry, or complicated mathematical formulas at the same level as a student who wants to become an engineer or mathematician. Similarly, students interested in languages, music, design, or other creative fields may benefit more from spending additional time developing skills related to their chosen areas. We also believe that students spend a large amount of time preparing for Mathematics examinations. Sometimes, students focus mainly on memorising formulas and solving examination-style questions instead of understanding how mathematics can actually be used in real life. Instead of making advanced Mathematics compulsory for every student, schools could provide a strong foundation in basic Mathematics and then offer different levels of Mathematics according to students' interests and future careers. Students who enjoy Mathematics or need advanced Mathematics for their future should have the opportunity to study subjects such as algebra, geometry, calculus, statistics, and advanced problem-solving. Other students could choose practical Mathematics, which could include financial literacy, budgeting, taxes, banking, percentages, data interpretation, measurements, and everyday calculations. This approach could give students more flexibility while still ensuring that everyone has the mathematical knowledge needed for daily life. We are not saying that Mathematics is completely useless. We understand that Mathematics is important for science, technology, engineering, economics, finance, and many other fields. Our request is that students should have a choice about how far they want to study it after learning the essential basics. Therefore, our humble request to the government is:

Please make suitable changes to the education system so that advanced Mathematics is not unnecessarily compulsory for every student. Ensure that every student learns essential mathematical skills, while allowing students to choose advanced Mathematics according to their interests, abilities, and future career goals.

Education should not force every student to follow exactly the same path. Students have different talents, dreams, and ambitions. Give us the basic Mathematics we need for life, and give us the freedom to choose how much more we want to learn.


7..... Not Want to Study for Marks Only

We, as students, have another important request regarding our education system. We believe that education should not be only about getting high marks in examinations. Education should help students gain knowledge, develop useful skills, understand the real world, and prepare for their future. Today, many students spend a large amount of time preparing for examinations. They often have to memorise chapters, definitions, formulas, and answers simply because they may appear in the examination. This can make learning feel like a race for marks rather than an opportunity to understand and explore new ideas. We believe that marks are useful for measuring academic progress, but marks should not be the only measure of a student's ability. A student may have excellent creativity, communication skills, leadership abilities, technical knowledge, problem-solving skills, or practical intelligence without always getting the highest marks in written examinations. Schools should give students more opportunities to learn through practical activities, projects, experiments, presentations, teamwork, and real-world challenges. For example, instead of only learning about business from a textbook, students could be given a small entrepreneurship project. Instead of only reading about computers, they could learn coding and create a simple application. Instead of only memorising scientific concepts, they could perform experiments and understand how those concepts work in real life. Students should also learn important life skills such as financial literacy, communication, digital safety, time management, basic first aid, critical thinking, and responsible use of technology. These skills can be extremely useful in everyday life and future careers. We also believe that examinations should test understanding rather than only memorisation. Questions should encourage students to think, analyse, create, and solve problems. This would help students develop skills that can be useful beyond the classroom. We are not saying that examinations or marks should completely disappear. They can still be an important part of education. However, they should be combined with practical learning and other ways of evaluating students. Therefore, our humble request to the government is:

Please make suitable changes to the education system so that students are not judged mainly by examination marks. Give greater importance to practical skills, creativity, problem-solving, projects, communication, and real-world learning.

Every student is different. Some students may be excellent at mathematics, while others may be talented in technology, sports, art, music, communication, business, or other areas. Education should not teach us only how to score marks. It should teach us how to use our knowledge in real life. We want an education system where students do not study only to pass an examination—they study to understand, create, innovate, and build their future.


8..... Want to Study Everything in Practical

We, as students, have another important request regarding our education system. We want our education to become more practical. We believe that students should not only read information from textbooks and memorise answers for examinations. We should also learn how things actually work in real life. In the current education system, students often spend many hours reading chapters, learning definitions, memorising formulas, and preparing written answers. Although theoretical knowledge is important, simply remembering information is not always enough. Students should get opportunities to apply what they learn. For example, instead of only reading about science experiments in a textbook, students should actually perform those experiments in a laboratory. Instead of only learning about computers from books, students should use computers and learn coding, programming, and other digital skills. Instead of only studying mathematics, students could learn how mathematics is used in banking, shopping, budgeting, construction, business, technology, and everyday life. The same approach can be applied to other subjects. In Geography, students could use maps, models, digital tools, and field activities. In Biology, students could observe plants, animals, cells, and ecosystems through practical activities. In languages, students could practise speaking, presentations, debates, and real-life communication instead of focusing only on written answers. Even subjects such as Economics and Business Studies can become more practical. Students could learn how to create a budget, manage money, understand banking, start a small project, or develop a simple business idea. Practical education can also help students discover their interests and talents. A student may not understand their abilities simply by reading a textbook, but when they are given an opportunity to build, create, experiment, communicate, or solve a real problem, they may discover what they enjoy. We also believe that practical learning can make education more interesting. Students may become more engaged when they can see how something works instead of only memorising information about it. However, we are not saying that textbooks and theory should completely disappear. Theory provides the foundation needed to understand many subjects. Our request is that theory should be combined with practical experience. Therefore, our humble request to the government is:

Please make practical learning an important part of every subject. Schools should provide laboratories, workshops, projects, field activities, technology, experiments, and real-world tasks so that students can apply what they learn.

Education should prepare students not only to answer questions on an examination paper but also to solve problems in real life. We don't want to learn only by reading. We want to learn by seeing, doing, creating, experimenting, and experiencing. Learn it. Do it. Understand it. Use it.


9..... Want to Learn How to Solve Life Problems

We, as students, have another important request regarding our education system. We want schools to teach us not only how to pass examinations, but also how to solve problems that we may face in real life. Every person faces different challenges in life. We may have to make important decisions, manage money, deal with difficult situations, communicate with other people, solve conflicts, handle emergencies, manage our time, and make decisions about our education and careers. However, students often receive very little practical training for these situations in school. We spend many years studying different subjects and preparing for examinations, but sometimes we are not taught how to apply our knowledge when we face a real-life problem. We believe that education should prepare us for both examinations and everyday life. For example, students should learn how to create a personal budget, save money, understand basic banking, recognise online scams, protect their personal information, and make responsible financial decisions. These are skills that can be useful throughout life. Students should also learn how to handle emergencies. Basic first aid, emergency communication, fire safety, road safety, and disaster preparedness could help students respond responsibly when something unexpected happens. Problem-solving should also be taught through practical activities. Instead of simply giving students questions with predetermined answers, teachers could give them real-world problems and ask them to find different solutions. Students could work in teams, discuss ideas, test solutions, learn from mistakes, and improve their results. Critical thinking should be an important part of this education. Students should learn how to identify a problem, understand its cause, collect reliable information, consider different solutions, and choose a responsible course of action. This approach could also help students become more confident and independent. They would learn that making mistakes is not always failure. Sometimes mistakes are an important part of learning and improving. Technology and Artificial Intelligence can also be used to teach problem-solving. Students could use digital tools to analyse problems, create projects, develop solutions, and understand how technology can be used to address real-world challenges. We are not asking schools to stop teaching traditional academic subjects. Mathematics, science, languages, and other subjects are important. Our request is that these subjects should also be connected with practical life whenever possible. Therefore, our humble request to the government is:

Please introduce more real-life problem-solving education in schools. Give students practical situations, projects, challenges, and activities that teach them how to think, make decisions, work with others, and solve problems independently.

Education should not only teach us what to know. It should also teach us how to think, what to do, and how to solve problems when life becomes difficult. We don't want to prepare only for tomorrow's examination. We want to prepare for tomorrow's life.


10..... Want to Add a Gym in School

We, as students, have another important request regarding our education system. We believe that education should focus not only on academic knowledge but also on the physical health and fitness of students. Therefore, we want schools to provide proper gym and fitness facilities for students. Students spend a large part of their day sitting in classrooms, studying, writing, using computers, and preparing for examinations. Because of this, many students do not get enough physical activity during the day. A healthy body is important for a healthy and active life, so schools should provide students with regular opportunities to exercise. A school gym could provide a safe and organised place where students can perform age-appropriate exercises under proper supervision. It could include basic fitness equipment, stretching areas, exercise mats, and other suitable facilities. Students could learn exercises that improve strength, flexibility, balance, stamina, and overall fitness. However, a school gym should not be focused only on building muscles or lifting heavy weights. It should mainly teach students about healthy physical activity and proper exercise habits. Qualified teachers or trained fitness instructors should guide students and make sure that exercises are appropriate for their age and physical development. Physical fitness can also teach students important qualities such as discipline, consistency, patience, teamwork, and goal-setting. Students can learn that improving fitness takes time and regular effort. A gym should also not replace outdoor sports. Activities such as running, football, cricket, basketball, badminton, athletics, and other sports are important because they provide physical activity while also developing teamwork and coordination. A school could provide both fitness facilities and sports facilities. We also believe that gym facilities should be accessible to all students, regardless of their financial background. If schools provide these facilities, students should not have to pay expensive membership fees to use them. Schools could create age-appropriate fitness programmes. Younger students could focus on stretching, movement, coordination, flexibility, and basic bodyweight exercises. Older students could gradually learn more structured fitness activities under proper supervision. Physical education should be treated as an important part of education rather than something that students do only when they have free time. Therefore, our humble request to the government is:

Please encourage schools to provide safe, age-appropriate gym and fitness facilities for students. Ensure that schools have trained staff, proper safety rules, suitable equipment, and regular physical-education programmes.

We want an education system that develops both our minds and our bodies. A strong education needs a healthy student. We don't want to study only with our minds—we also want to keep our bodies active, healthy, and fit.
